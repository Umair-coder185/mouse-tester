Your aim feels off, and you can’t explain why. Flicks overshoot, slow moves fall short, and muscle memory never settles. The culprit is often pointer precision, the Windows setting behind mouse acceleration. Short answer: switch it off for gaming and keep it on for office work. Here’s why that split works, plus the exact steps.

> **Quick Verdict:** Turn pointer precision off for FPS and other competitive games. Keep it on for office work, browsing and photo editing. For RTS and MOBA games, start with it off and switch only if big sweeps tire your hand.

## Pointer Precision Is Just Mouse Acceleration With a Friendlier Name

Windows labels the feature Enhance Pointer Precision, but it’s mouse acceleration. It changes cursor speed based on how fast your hand moves, not only how far. ## Pointer Precision Breaks the 1:1 Link Between Hand and Cursor

With pointer precision off, Windows keeps 1:1 mapping. Move the mouse five centimeters and the cursor travels the same distance every time, whether you move slowly or quickly. Your hand learns that distance without effort.

Turn pointer precision on and speed enters the equation. A slow drag produces a short cursor travel, while a fast flick sends the cursor much farther. The physical movement is identical, yet the result changes. You can test your [mouse movement](https://www.clickmousetest.com/) patterns to feel the difference firsthand.

| Behavior | Pointer Precision Off | Pointer Precision On |
|---|---|---|
| Cursor distance | Same for slow and fast moves | Grows with hand speed |
| Muscle memory | Builds quickly | Builds slowly |
| Tiny adjustments | Predictable | Very precise |
| Large screen sweeps | Needs more desk space | Needs less desk space |
| Best for | Competitive gaming | Office and creative work |

## Should You Turn Off Pointer Precision for Gaming? Yes, in Most Cases

Competitive players want consistency. In FPS games like Valorant, your hand must know exactly how far to travel to reach a head. Acceleration blurs that knowledge, so players who chase accuracy generally prefer pointer precision off.

Then comes over-aiming. An enemy appears behind you, you flick hard, and the crosshair flies past the target. You correct with micro-adjustments, and that delay costs rounds. Note that games with raw mouse input ignore the Windows setting, and Riot Games offers a Raw Input Buffer option in Valorant for that reason. Always check your in-game mouse acceleration and mouse smoothing options too.

## Pointer Precision Still Helps With Office Work, 4K Screens and RTS Games

Daily computing is a different story. Pointer precision lets you nudge a tiny button slowly, then fling the cursor across a 4K display with one wrist flick. On multi-monitor setups, that saves real desk space and reduces hand fatigue during long office work, web browsing and photo editing sessions.

RTS games and MOBA games sit in the middle. StarCraft II and League of Legends demand quick camera sweeps and precise unit selection. Some advanced players like a mild curve, but it takes practice. Begin with pointer precision off, then switch it on if large movements tire your hand.

## How to Turn Off Pointer Precision in Windows 11 and Windows 10

Microsoft keeps this toggle in the classic Windows mouse settings, so the steps match on both versions. Follow them in order:

1. Open Settings.
2. Go to Bluetooth & devices, then Mouse (on Windows 10, open Devices, then Mouse).
3. Click Additional mouse settings.
4. Open the Pointer Options tab.
5. Uncheck Enhance pointer precision.
6. Click Apply, then OK.

Once you disable Enhance Pointer Precision, set the pointer speed slider to the sixth notch out of eleven, the Windows default. That position keeps movement linear, so your mouse sensitivity comes purely from your DPI and in-game settings.

## How to Change Mouse Acceleration on a Mac

macOS has no simple pointer precision toggle like Windows. You can open System Settings, choose Mouse, and lower Tracking speed, as Apple Support describes. However, that only changes the overall speed and doesn’t remove the acceleration curve.

For a truly flat response, you’ll need mouse acceleration software. Utilities such as LinearMouse or SteerMouse let you disable the curve. Afterward, run the sweep test below to confirm your macOS mouse acceleration is gone.

## Pointer Precision vs DPI vs Polling Rate: Three Separate Settings

Many players mix these up, so let’s separate them. Mouse DPI sets base sensitivity, mouse polling rate decides how often the mouse reports its position (which you can check with the [Polling Rate Test](https://www.clickmousetest.com/polling-rate-test)), and pointer precision decides whether speed changes distance.

| Setting | What It Controls | Impact | Suggested Choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pointer precision | Cursor behavior | Consistency | Off for gaming |
| DPI | Cursor speed | Sensitivity | Personal comfort |
| Polling rate | Input frequency | Responsiveness | Highest stable rate your mouse supports |

Pick a DPI that lets you land on small targets without strain, then stick with it. Use in-game sensitivity for fine tuning. The [DPI Analyzer](https://www.clickmousetest.com/mouse-dpi-analyzer) helps you confirm that your mouse reports the number printed on the box.

## How to Test Mouse Acceleration in Under Two Minutes

Start with the sweep test, a simple cursor consistency test. Place the cursor at a fixed point, drag the mouse slowly from left to right, then repeat the same distance quickly. If the cursor lands in two different places, acceleration is still active.

Next, back it up with data. The [Mouse Test](https://www.clickmousetest.com/) shows mouse movement patterns and exposes odd jumps. The [Polling Rate Test](https://www.clickmousetest.com/polling-rate-test) confirms your input behavior is stable at 1000Hz or higher. Finally, enable raw input and disable smoothing inside your game.

## 7 Smart Tips for Setting Up Pointer Precision

Small habits make the difference between a clean setup and a frustrating one. Use these tips as a checklist.

### 1. Match the Setting to the Task
Gaming rewards predictability, while desktop work rewards speed. Decide which job your mouse does most, then choose pointer precision accordingly.

### 2. Lock Your DPI First
Changing DPI and acceleration together makes problems impossible to trace. Fix your DPI with the [DPI Analyzer](https://www.clickmousetest.com/mouse-dpi-analyzer), then adjust one thing at a time.

### 3. Keep Pointer Speed at 6 of 11
This is the Windows default and the linear position. Moving it up or down alters the scaling and can add inconsistency.

### 4. Disable In-Game Acceleration and Smoothing
Some titles add their own curve on top of Windows. Set acceleration to zero and turn raw input on wherever it exists.

### 5. Give Your Hand Time to Adapt
Switching pointer precision off feels strange at first, especially for flick aiming. Practice in a training range until your crosshair placement feels natural again.

### 6. Retest After Driver Updates
Mouse software can change settings quietly. Run the sweep test after any update so surprises don’t reach a ranked match.

### 7. Use Separate Profiles for Work and Play
If your mouse software supports profiles, keep one with acceleration for work and one without for gaming. You’ll switch in seconds.

## FAQs

### Does turning off mouse acceleration improve aim?

Yes, for most players. It gives you consistent cursor accuracy, which makes muscle memory easier to build.

### Should Enhance Pointer Precision be on or off?

Off for competitive gaming, on for office work and browsing. RTS and MOBA players can choose by comfort.

### Is mouse acceleration bad for gaming?

In most competitive games, yes. It makes the same hand movement produce different results.

### Does the Mac have mouse acceleration?

Yes, macOS uses an acceleration curve by default. Removing it fully requires a third-party utility.

### Why does my mouse feel inconsistent?

Common causes are acceleration, unstable DPI or low polling rate. If clicks misfire instead, run the [Double Click Test](https://www.clickmousetest.com/double-click-test).

## Conclusion

Pointer precision isn’t good or bad. It’s a tool, and the right choice depends on your task. Gaming needs a fixed relationship between hand and cursor. Desktop work benefits from speed-based movement.

Windows makes the switch easy: open Pointer Options, uncheck Enhance pointer precision, and keep your pointer speed at the default. Mac users need a third-party utility for a fully flat response. Then check your result with the sweep test and verify sensor tracking with the [Mouse Test](https://www.clickmousetest.com/).

Finally, remember that DPI and polling rate work independently. Choose a comfortable DPI, keep the highest stable polling rate, and let in-game sensitivity handle the fine tuning. Give yourself a few sessions to adapt, and your aim will start to feel steady.
